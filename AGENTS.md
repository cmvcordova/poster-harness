# Poster editing

- This template is reusable across conferences. Keep the default format unless the user or conference requirements call for changes.
- Read README.md first. Edit content.tex for text, authors, figures, references, URLs, and affiliations.
- For a new research poster, read brief.md and fill it from the user's request. Ask together for missing source material or requirements needed to proceed; use the stated defaults and infer author information from the paper. Optional preferences may stay blank. Work in the new poster folder, keeping the reusable template unchanged.
- Fill `outline.md` from the brief and sources before editing `content.tex`, and show the proposed outline to the user. Plan the center takeaway first, then the left standalone overview, right discussion evidence, and footer. Record `Beat`, `Content`, optional `Visual`, and precise `Source` fields for each block; keep them current as the poster changes. Resolve missing evidence rather than inventing claims. The outline is editorial guidance, not a parsed build input.
- Edit poster-style.sty only for layout, fonts, colors, spacing, or paper dimensions. Default: 48 x 36 inches, landscape; pane widths are 20% left / 60% center / 20% right.
- Preserve the default print typography defined by the Poster*Font commands: 41.76 pt side body, 56.73 pt center body (derived from the shared heading/body ratio), 51.04 pt side headings, 48.72 pt paper title, 208.13 pt takeaway, and 96 pt subtitle. Shorten content rather than reducing these sizes.
- Set font sizes in points using `\SideTitleFontSize`, `\SideHeadingFontSize`, and `\SideBodyFontSize` (shared by left/right), plus `\CenterTitleFontSize`, `\CenterHeadingFontSize`, and `\CenterBodyFontSize` (center/footer). Adjust each role independently when requested; defaults use the standard template sizes and derive center body as center heading * side body / side heading. Captions and small text follow their pane.
- Keep this folder standalone: copy required assets into assets/; never reference files outside this folder.
- Discover content from paths and URLs recorded in brief.md under “What exists, and where” and “Material to reuse”, including material discoverable from those locations. There is no fixed list of content folders to scan automatically; do not treat neighboring projects as inputs.
- In the new poster folder, keep copied source material and data in sources/, maintain sources/citations.json as a keyed register of titles, authors when available, original paths/URLs, and local files, keep reused or new figure scripts in figures/, and keep display images and generated figures in assets/. Create these source/script folders as needed. Original paths/URLs are provenance, not external build dependencies; copy everything needed to reproduce figures locally and retain precise evidence locators in outline.md.
- Consider older posters or talks only when referenced in the brief or discovered through its listed sources. Copy relevant data/sources into sources/ and figure scripts into figures/, retaining attribution. Never reuse their _src/ files or poster layout files. The reusable template's content.tex, main.tex, poster-style.sty, and example assets are layout/design references, not sources of research content or results.
- Read the supplied paper and included TeX sections. Preserve claims, numbers, figure labels, and attribution. Never invent results or citations.
- Write for three audiences, following the supplied BetterPoster reference:
  - Center: people walking past. State the main finding in one or two plain-language sentences, readable at a glance. Lead with the conclusion and why it matters. Keep the takeaway dominant; any central figure should make it easier to understand immediately.
  - Left: people who stop to read without speaking to the presenter. Include the paper title, authors/affiliations, a self-contained summary, and a brief method. Cover the question, contribution, strongest supported finding, and relevance. Use short bullets, selective bold emphasis, and only essential jargon. Aim for about one minute of reading; make it useful in a photo taken for later.
  - Right: people discussing the work with the presenter. Prioritize experiments, labeled figures, comparisons, and compact tables that support the conversation. Keep explanatory prose minimal, retain necessary conditions/units/uncertainty, and include limitations where they affect interpretation. Section choices are flexible; avoid cramming in the whole paper.
- Preserve the default footer: two 5.8-inch QR images with inward-facing labels/outward arrows and a centered Mila/McGill logo row. Set file macros in content.tex; positions and sizes live in PosterFooterLayout. Replace both example QR destinations.
- The selected purple `#662E7D` is Mila's official logo purple, used by `assets/logos/mila-color.pdf`, sourced from https://mila.quebec/sites/default/themes/mila_v1/logo.svg . Use this exact value consistently for purple headings, accents, and QR palettes unless the user requests another color.
- The pale green `#B9DBC1` comes from Mila's official website stylesheet: https://mila.quebec/sites/default/files/css/css_J4CCfVN5SWjedZp9IH4f0a8J8wc57536YUd18MesaZQ.css . It appears in theme-green backgrounds and ckeditor-style-theme-green bullets. Use it for the left-pane background unless requested otherwise; describe it as a website color, not a formally published brand-standard specification.
- QR generation inputs: only the destination URL is required. Filename is optional (default qr.png); mode is optional (default light: purple on white; dark: white on purple). For a paper/code request, use paper-qr.png and code-qr.png to avoid overwriting either code. Default to light unless a dark background is selected; infer filenames rather than asking for optional inputs. Update PaperQRFile/CodeQRFile in content.tex, then run make check and inspect the PDF.
- Generate QR codes locally with `python3 scripts/make-qr.py URL paper-qr.png --mode light` (or `--mode dark`). The script always saves to `assets/qrcodes/` and accepts only a PNG filename, never a directory/path. No filename defaults to `assets/qrcodes/qr.png`. QR generation and build/check/clean require no package installation. The QR encoder is bundled in `scripts/vendor/` with its MIT license; preserve it when copying scripts. Light palette: #662E7D foreground / #FFFFFF background; dark palette: #FFFFFF foreground / #662E7D background. Update PaperQRFile and CodeQRFile in content.tex and scan-test final assets.
- Place a working paper/code QR and appropriate institution logos in the center footer. Verify the encoded destination matches the displayed link. Choose logo variants for contrast with the actual background.
- Preserve whitespace and the reading hierarchy: takeaway first, standalone overview second, discussion details third. References/acknowledgments should be compact. Example instructions and QR destination must be replaced before publication.
- Use \PosterSection, \PosterBullets, \PosterFigure, \PosterReferences, and \PosterQR. Preserve figure aspect ratios.
- Run make check. Fix overflowing panels or boxes by shortening content or resizing figures before reducing body type.
- Render output/pdf/main.pdf and inspect the entire page for clipping, overlap, readability, and figure quality. Check QR destinations and logos. Compilation alone is insufficient.
- Update README.md when the build or file structure changes. Do not commit build logs or source paper folders.

- Keep logo assets in PDF or PNG format for direct pdfLaTeX use. Prefer an existing PDF; otherwise use an original PNG. Preserve source URLs in assets/LOGO-SOURCES.md. Do not recolor or convert newly downloaded logos merely to provide extra formats.
